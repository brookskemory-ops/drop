# Finding Your Own Winners — The Durable Skill

Any product list (including mine) goes stale. The skill that keeps paying off is being able
to **find and validate a winning product yourself.** Here's the repeatable loop.

## What a "winning product" actually is

Not "the most viral thing." A winner is a product that hits **most** of these:

- **Solves a real, visible problem** or delivers an instant "wow" on camera.
- **3x+ markup possible** — costs $5–15, credibly sells for $25–45+.
- **Lightweight, durable, cheap to ship** (lower shipping cost, fewer breakage returns).
- **Not already in every big-box store** — you need a reason people buy from *you*.
- **Demand is rising or steady**, not a spike that already peaked.
- **Hard to price-compare** — bundles, personalization, or a branded angle protect margin.

## Where to look for candidates

- **TikTok / Reels:** search your niche + "#tiktokmademebuyit". Watch what's getting real
  engagement (comments asking "where do I get this"), not just view counts.
- **TikTok Creative Center:** shows trending products, ads, and formats by region.
- **Amazon Movers & Shakers / Best Sellers** in your niche category.
- **Supplier trend pages:** AliExpress, CJdropshipping, Doba, Zendrop, Syncee publish
  "trending" and "winning product" lists updated monthly.
- **Product research tools** (paid, optional): Minea, WinningHunter, Sell The Trend,
  Dropship.io — they surface what's spending on ads right now.

## The validation checklist (do this before spending real money)

1. **Google Trends** — search the product. Is interest rising, flat, or already crashed?
   Avoid anything past its peak.
2. **Ad activity** — are other sellers actively running ads on it? Some competition is
   *good* (proves demand). Zero competition often means zero demand.
3. **Margin math** — get the real landed cost (product + shipping). Can you sell at 3x and
   still leave room for ad spend? If not, skip.
4. **Content test** — can *you* make one genuinely scroll-stopping video with it? If you
   can't imagine the hook, your customers won't see one either.
5. **Order it to yourself** — quality, packaging, and actual shipping time. Non-negotiable.
6. **Saturation check** — is it already in every big-box store / sold by 500 identical
   stores? If so, you need a real differentiator (angle, bundle, brand) or a different product.

## Reading demand signals without fooling yourself

- **Views ≠ demand.** Comments like "need this" / "where to buy" are the real signal.
- **A spike is a warning, not a green light** — by the time something is everywhere, the
  early margin is gone. Look for *rising* trends you can get in front of.
- **Seasonality is real.** Check whether demand is year-round or tied to weather/holidays,
  and plan launch timing accordingly.

## Testing protocol (keep it cheap)

- Test **one product at a time** with a tight creative loop.
- Judge a product on **30–50 videos**, not 3. Content is the variable you're really testing.
- Change **one thing at a time** (hook, price, offer) so you know what moved the numbers.
- Set a kill rule *before* you start (e.g. "no traction after 40 videos and 3 weeks → kill").
  Deciding in advance stops you from throwing good money after a dead product.

## Common beginner mistakes to avoid

- Building a 50-product store instead of testing one hero product.
- Picking products by "coolness" with no margin or no problem-solved.
- Lying/vague about shipping times → chargebacks and bad reviews.
- Quitting after one product. **The winner is usually product #3–5, not #1.**
- Spending on paid ads before proving the product organically.
- Making health/medical claims on wellness products (legal risk).
