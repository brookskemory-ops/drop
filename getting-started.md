# Getting Started: Your First 30 Days

A step-by-step launch plan. Do these in order. Don't skip step 1 to jump to "cool products" —
the product only wins inside a niche you understand.

---

## Step 1 — Pick a niche you actually get (Day 1–2)

Choose a niche you already understand as a *customer*. If you own pets, lift weights, or
struggle with sleep, you already know the language, the pain points, and what a good ad
looks like. That's a real edge over someone chasing whatever's trending blind.

Good beginner niches in 2026 (all backed by durable demand, not fads):

- **Home comfort & wellness** — people replacing expensive services (spa, gym, therapy)
  with cheap at-home versions.
- **Pets** — personalized pet accessories had the single strongest demand signal in
  Summer 2026 data.
- **Work-from-anywhere tech** — remote work is the default; portable, professional gear sells.
- **Beauty & skincare** — ingredient-driven trends (e.g. PDRN) are expanding globally.

Pick **one**. You can always launch a second store later.

## Step 2 — Choose one product to test first (Day 2–4)

Don't build a 50-product store. Pick **one hero product**. See
[`product-shortlist.md`](./product-shortlist.md) for vetted starting points, and
[`product-research.md`](./product-research.md) for how to validate your own.

Criteria for a testable product:
- Costs you **$5–15**, sells for **$25–45** (3x+ markup / 30–65% margin).
- Solves a visible problem OR has an obvious "wow" moment on camera.
- Lightweight and not fragile (cheaper shipping, fewer returns).
- Not sold in every big-box store already (you need a reason to buy from *you*).

## Step 3 — Set up your store (Day 4–7)

Two paths — pick based on where your customers are:

| Path | Best when | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| **TikTok Shop** | You'll drive sales with TikTok video | In-app checkout, lowest friction. Requires business docs (registration, bank account, tax info, ID) to get approved. |
| **Shopify** | You want full control + multi-channel | Shopify Starter plan is built for social selling; works with TikTok, Instagram, etc. |

Recommendation for a beginner: **start with TikTok Shop** if you'll make video content
(you should), because checkout happens where the attention is. Add Shopify later for control.

Minimum viable store:
- Clean logo (a wordmark is fine — use Canva).
- One hero product page with 5–7 real photos + a demo video.
- Trust basics: clear shipping times, a refund policy, a contact email. **Be honest about
  shipping times** — this is the #1 source of chargebacks and bad reviews.

## Step 4 — Find a supplier (Day 5–8)

Order the product to **yourself first**. Always. You cannot sell something you haven't held.

Common supplier sources: AliExpress, CJdropshipping, Zendrop, Doba, Syncee, Spocket.
For faster shipping (a big trust win), prefer suppliers with **US/EU warehouses**.

Vet a supplier on:
- Shipping time to your customers' country (aim < 10 days).
- Reviews and order volume (avoid brand-new listings).
- Whether they'll do **blind shipping** (no supplier invoice in the package).

## Step 5 — Make content before you spend a dollar on ads (Day 8–20)

This is the actual business in 2026. Paid ads are expensive and unforgiving for beginners;
**organic short-form video is where you learn what converts.**

- Film the product solving its problem. Show the "wow" moment in the first 2 seconds.
- Post **daily**. Plan for **30–50 videos** before you judge whether a product works.
- Use TikTok Creative Center to study top-performing formats in your niche.
- When one video gets traction, make 5 more variations of it.

## Step 6 — Launch, measure, and read the numbers honestly (Day 20–30)

Watch these, in order:
1. **Are videos getting views?** No views = content problem, not product problem.
2. **Are viewers clicking to the product?** Low clicks = weak hook or offer.
3. **Are clickers buying?** Traffic but no sales = price, trust, or product-page problem.

Each failure points to a *specific* fix. Don't blow up the whole store — change one variable.

## Step 7 — Kill or scale (Day 30+)

- **No traction after 30–50 videos and a real effort?** Kill it. Keep the store and niche,
  swap the product. This is normal — treat it as a paid experiment, not a failure.
- **Getting consistent sales?** Now scale: more content, then paid ads to amplify your
  best-performing organic videos, then add complementary products.

> Reality check: most sellers who pass **$1,000/month** have been running for **3+ months**
> and posted **30–50+ videos**. The people who quit at week 2 are why "dropshipping doesn't
> work" is a common belief. Persistence past the awkward early phase is the moat.
