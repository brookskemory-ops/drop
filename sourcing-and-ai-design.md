# Sourcing + AI Design — Where to Print and How to Make Artwork

Two separate jobs: (1) the **printer + blanks** that produce your shirts, and (2) the **AI
tools** that create your front/back artwork. This covers both, tuned for men's premium
heavyweight basics printed via POD (Phase 1). Real cut-and-sew manufacturing comes in Phase 2.

---

## Part 1 — Printer + blanks (your Phase 1 "manufacturer")

For POD you're not hiring a manufacturer yet — you're choosing a **print-on-demand provider**
that stocks **premium heavyweight blanks** and supports **multi-placement printing** (the
industry term for **front + back**). Ranked for men's premium streetwear basics:

| Provider | Why it fits | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| **Tapstitch** ⭐ | Built for premium streetwear: heavyweight tees & oversized hoodies **up to 430 GSM**, DTG **+** DTF, **multi-placement** (front/back/neck) | Shopify is their flagship integration — best match for this niche |
| **Apliiq** | Streetwear focus; premium blanks (Bella+Canvas, **AS Colour**); custom **woven neck labels** for real brand feel | Best when you want branded labeling, not just prints |
| **Gelato** | Front/back/sleeve printing; 100+ partners in 200+ countries → fast local delivery | Strong for shipping speed/trust |
| **Printful** | Most reliable/polished; best integrations; solid blanks | More generic; good backup/second source |

**Blank to request:** heavyweight cotton **280–400+ GSM** — e.g. **AS Colour** heavyweight
(the **5161** is a go-to for high-end streetwear) or Tapstitch 400–430 GSM tees. Weight is the
#1 quality signal for men's basics. **Order samples and physically feel them before committing.**

**DTG vs DTF (print methods):**
- **DTG** (direct-to-garment): softer, blends into the fabric; best for detailed/photographic art.
- **DTF** (direct-to-film transfer): more durable and vivid; great for bold graphics and colors.
- Most premium providers offer both — pick per design; sample both to see what you prefer.

> ⚠️ **Margin note:** each print **placement is charged separately**, so front + back costs more
> than a single print. That's why big-back/small-front streetwear still needs to retail at $40+.
> Factor both placements into your 4x-landed-cost pricing.

**Phase 2 (later) — real manufacturers:** when you move winners to cut-and-sew (technical
shorts, performance tees), look at low-MOQ activewear factories (50–100 units/style) via
sourcing platforms (e.g. Alibaba verified suppliers, Pietra) — see
[`gym-brand.md`](./gym-brand.md) for MOQ/fabric/tech-pack guidance.

## Part 2 — AI design tools (front & back)

**Key mental model:** AI generates the *artwork*; you then make it **print-ready** (transparent
PNG, 300 DPI, correct size). No single tool does all of it perfectly — use the right one per job.

| Tool | Best for | Watch out |
|------|----------|-----------|
| **Midjourney (v7)** | Best raw artistic/graphic output; distinctive style that stands out | No native transparent background — remove it afterward |
| **Ideogram** | **Text/typography** — wordmarks, slogans, brand name; most accurate AI text | Use whenever the design has words |
| **Kittl** ⭐ | All-in-one for merch: AI generate → **vectorize** → text effects → print-ready vector export | Best "make it usable" tool; minor learning curve |
| **MerchBanao** | Production flow: generate + background removal + upscale + **300 DPI transparent PNG** in one session | Optimized for volume |
| **Canva** | Fastest for beginners; templates + Magic Media AI; easy mockups | Less unique output |

**The AI-text problem:** image models still garble letters. For anything with words (most gym
apparel — brand name, slogans), use **Ideogram**, or generate the graphic in Midjourney and
**add the text yourself** in Kittl/Canva so it's crisp and on-brand.

### Recommended workflow
1. **Concept** — describe the design idea (aesthetic, mood, subject, colors).
2. **Generate** — Midjourney for graphic art; Ideogram for text-driven designs.
3. **Make print-ready** — remove background + upscale to **300 DPI transparent PNG** (Kittl or
   MerchBanao). For bold, simple designs, **vectorize** in Kittl so it stays sharp at any size.
4. **Place front & back as a pair** — classic premium streetwear layout is **small front**
   (left-chest logo or small centered mark) + **large back** (the statement graphic). Upload
   each placement as its own file.

### Front vs. back design guidance
- **Front:** keep it subtle — left-chest logo (~3–4") or a small centered mark. Premium/minimal
  men's basics rarely have a huge front print.
- **Back:** your big canvas — the statement graphic, ~12–14" wide. This front-small/back-big
  split is the streetwear standard and reads as premium.
- Design the two together so they feel like one cohesive piece, not two random graphics.

### Print specs to hand the printer
- **Format:** transparent **PNG**, **300 DPI** (standard for DTG/DTF).
- **Back (large):** ~12–14" wide → build the file ~**3600–4200px** wide.
- **Front (chest):** small — ~3–4" logo or small centered mark.
- For bold/simple designs, keep a **vector** master so you can scale to any garment/size.

### Two things to be careful about
- **Commercial rights:** use a **paid plan that grants commercial use** (Midjourney's paid plans
  do). **Never** generate anything mimicking existing brands, logos, characters, or real people —
  that's a takedown/lawsuit risk.
- **AI ≠ perfect first try:** budget a little cleanup (Kittl, or free Photopea) to fix edges,
  contrast, and text before it's truly print-ready.

---

**Bottom line:** start with **Tapstitch** (or Apliiq) on a **heavyweight AS Colour / 400+ GSM
blank** with front+back placement, and design with **Midjourney (art) + Ideogram (text) → Kittl
(make print-ready)**. Order a sample of your first design and wear-test it before you launch.
