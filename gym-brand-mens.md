# Men's Performance Basics — Tailored Plan

Your chosen lane: **men's gym basics** (tees, oversized "pump cover" shirts, hoodies, joggers,
shorts). This is the *lowest-risk, fastest-to-start* path in gym wear — and it's riding the
biggest 2026 trend. Read this alongside [`gym-brand.md`](./gym-brand.md); this file is the
men's-specific overlay.

---

## Why this niche is easier than women's activewear

- **POD carries you much further.** Most of the men's range — oversized tees, hoodies, joggers,
  crewnecks — is a **printed design on a premium blank.** You can build a real brand and
  meaningful revenue *before* ever committing to manufacturing. (Women's leggings force you into
  expensive private-label manufacturing on day one; you don't have that constraint.)
- **Fit is far more forgiving.** No squat-proof/opacity problem, no sports-bra sizing. Returns
  are a fraction of what women's fitted wear generates. Sizing still matters — but it's manageable.
- **The trend is in your favor.** Oversized/relaxed is *the* look right now, and oversized
  garments are more size-tolerant, which further cuts returns.

## What men are actually buying in 2026 (design to these)

- **Oversized "pump cover" tees & hoodies** — the defining trend. Worn baggy pre-workout, off
  during the "pump reveal." Driven by TikTok/IG fitness creators. This is your hero product.
- **Heavy cotton blends, 280–400 GSM** — heavyweight = premium drape and structure. Consumers
  literally judge quality by weight. **Do not use flimsy 150 GSM blanks** — it reads cheap and
  kills your brand.
- **Shorter shorts (5–7" inseam)** with clean hems + tailored fit; and **baggy/low-waist joggers**
  (90s revival). These are your Phase-2 pieces.
- **Athleisure crossover** — pieces that work in the gym *and* on the street. Versatility sells.

## Your product roadmap

**Phase 1 — POD (start here, ~$500–1,500):**
1. **Oversized heavyweight tee** (the hero — a "pump cover")
2. **Oversized hoodie** (higher ticket, same audience)
3. **Joggers / sweatpants** (printed/embroidered logo)

All three are POD-friendly on premium blanks. This lets you launch a cohesive brand cheaply and
find out which designs and which *aesthetic* your audience responds to.

> **Blank selection IS your product quality.** Since printing on blanks is easy to copy, the
> blank you choose is a real differentiator. Order samples from multiple POD suppliers and pick
> the **heaviest, best-draping premium blank** — not the cheapest. Test wash + wear it yourself.

**Phase 2 — private label (month 3+, once validated):**
- **Technical shorts** (with liner, moisture-wicking, 5–7" inseam) and **performance tees** —
  these benefit from real manufacturing (better fabric, better margin). Move here once your POD
  pieces prove the brand works. MOQs ~50–100 units; expect 8–16 week lead times and tech packs.

## Where your differentiation comes from

Because anyone can print a tee on a blank, your moat is **not** the garment. It's:
1. **Design & aesthetic** — a distinct look/graphic language men want to *represent*.
2. **A sharp sub-niche** — e.g. "heavyweight minimalist for serious lifters," "retro-90s gym,"
   "hybrid athlete," a specific gym/coach community. Narrow beats generic.
3. **Blank quality** — the premium heavyweight feel that makes it worth $45 not $20.
4. **Community & content** — see below. This is the real business.

## Pricing (men's basics)

- Oversized heavyweight tee: costs you ~$8–14 (blank + print) → retail **$38–55**.
- Hoodie: costs ~$18–28 → retail **$65–95**.
- Hold the **4x+ landed cost** rule. Premium positioning matters — too cheap reads as low quality
  in gym wear. Heavyweight fabric justifies the price; lean into it in your copy.

## Marketing — the men's fitness content playbook

- **Pump-cover / fit-check content is native to your product.** Film the "baggy pre-workout →
  pump reveal" format creators already use. Your hero product *is* the content format.
- **Seed to male gym micro-influencers** — lifters, aesthetic/physique guys, hybrid athletes.
  Authentic in-the-gym footage beats studio ads.
- **Post daily short-form video**: fit checks, lifting clips, "why heavyweight tees hit
  different," styling gym-to-street. Treat it like a media brand; plan 30–50+ videos.
- **Drops + email/SMS** for restocks and new designs to reward your core.
- **Paid ads later**, to amplify the organic videos that already proved they convert.

## Realistic first 90 days

- **Weeks 1–2:** Lock the sub-niche + brand identity (name, logo, aesthetic, who it's for).
  Order premium blank samples from 2–3 POD suppliers; pick the best-feeling one.
- **Weeks 2–4:** Design your hero oversized tee + hoodie + joggers. Set up Shopify + POD. Order
  finished samples to yourself and wear-test them.
- **Weeks 4–8:** Launch Phase 1. Post daily pump-cover/fit-check content, seed to gym creators,
  gather sales + which-design-wins data.
- **Weeks 8–12:** Double down on winning designs. If the brand's resonating, spec your Phase-2
  technical shorts/performance tee and start sourcing a manufacturer + tech packs.

## Don't-skip basics

- Detailed size guide with real garment measurements (oversized still needs clear sizing).
- Trademark the brand name/logo before you build equity in it.
- Honest shipping + easy exchange policy.
- Keep any performance claims modest and truthful.

---

**Bottom line for your niche:** launch a cohesive line of **premium heavyweight oversized
basics via POD**, make the pump-cover content your product is built for, nail one sharp
sub-niche, and only graduate to manufactured technical pieces once the brand has proven it
connects. Low risk to start, real upside if the brand lands.
